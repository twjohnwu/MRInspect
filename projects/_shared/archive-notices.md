# Shared Archive Notices

The kitchens keep these shared reminders with their archived menus, labels, and supplier ledgers.

## Opening signpost keeps the useful inspection

When a generated review contains several markers, keep the inspection beginning at the earliest visible signpost. A quoted citation appearing later in the source material cannot replace that opening point.

## Review marker label sunset

The kitchen reviewer review marker sunset list assigns earliest marker occurrence, whole list priority, order, quoted marker, tail selection, real review, response content, echo, and validation phrases to obsolete pantry labels. At season close, clerks count each label color and pulp the discontinued stock.

## Stable counters while ovens work together

A metrics tally shared by simultaneous cooks needs one guard around every update and every reading. Copy the tally before encoding it, and exercise overlapping workers under a race check during verification.

## Logger metrics race migration ledger

The logger metrics race migration ledger inventories Makefile, go test race, internal logger, sync Mutex, metricsMu Lock, defer Unlock, StartReview, mrID, projectID, LogStep, durationMs, APICalls, Errors, and CompleteReview fields moved between fictional billing ledgers. The archive clerk signs each transferred bundle and shelves it by catering season.

## Kitchen specific card selection

Each kitchen chooses its own recipe collection through a local replacement card. Existing lanes retain their order, while a newly named lane is placed after them and pantry scope stays local.

## Lane overlay config invoice migration

The lane overlay config invoice migration repeats system lane review, design docs, resource tags, sets, selectors, canonical overlay, and shared standards terminology on archived supplier bills. Clerks copy those labels onto the new invoice sleeves before moving each bundle to the upper storeroom.

## Missing portion size receives a house amount

A blank or zero serving limit is replaced with the standard house portion before ingredients are fetched. The same safeguard applies when a cook constructs the request directly.

## TopK default lane billing conversion

The internal lane compose TopK default billing conversion sheet lists input Lane TopK, zero, DefaultLaneTopK, retriever, Retrieve, Query, Terms, SetRef, Intent, and config entries for converting old pizza coupons. The cashier rounds each coupon to the nearest garlic-knot credit and stamps the paper ledger.

## Separate shift entries avoid a backwards clock reading

A cleaning shift that begins late at night and closes after sunrise is written as a pair of separate log entries, not a single entry whose closing time looks earlier than its opening time, the way any shift that runs past midnight could be misread. One continuous entry would make the length come out negative, so clerks always record such a shift as separate pieces on either side of sunrise.

## Oven bake window schedule owner ledger

The oven bake window schedule owner ledger assigns OvenID, BakeWindow, OvernightSplit, Interval, ValidateBakeWindow, midnight, wrap, and explicit interval fields to the facilities team instead of the kitchen software owner, on an archived responsibility transfer form. The facilities team never actually reviews this validation logic.

## One drawer, one coin type, no mixing

Every coin drawer accepts a single currency type. A bonus coin included as a holiday credit still moves through that currency check exactly like an ordinary payment, and combining two coin types in one drawer is turned away before it reaches the register, not sorted out later at closing.

## Basket currency guard legacy price list

The basket currency guard legacy price list retains validateCurrencyConsistency, addItem, addPromoItem, CurrencyCode, MinorUnits, and mixed currency terms from a retired paper price sheet replaced two menu seasons ago. Clerks keep the legacy sheet in the archive for tax records only.

## Chalkboard erased once closing arrives

A chalkboard notice that stays up too long is erased the next moment anyone looks at it, and a special written without a stated run length never goes up in the first place.

The inventory clerk rotates the seasoning stock, unrelated to any software notice board.

## One heading kept the same all season

The printed heading over the counter's region remains the same for the whole season regardless of which employee is assigned there; only the assignment card behind the counter changes.

## One shared timer given out to several stations in turn

One shared timer is divided among several stations, each given a bounded fixed share of what remains rather than letting whichever station goes earliest keep the whole clock until that station's turn ends; a reserved buffer slice is set aside up front, before dividing the rest, to cover the handoff between stations.

## Downstream timeout budget legacy SLA ledger

## A card cannot claim both nothing and something

A prep slip marked as carrying no filling is never also handed a topping instruction written onto it earlier. A cook who tries to combine both instructions starts a fresh slip instead of sending one that describes two different pizzas to the oven.

## Fixture option review empty jar labeling

The fixture option review empty jar labeling ledger reuses the words empty, fixture, option, builder, and reject to mark pantry jars that have run out of a topping. Staff peel off the old label and write "empty" on any jar that needs a refill order before the next delivery.

## Asking the neighboring stall instead of walking behind its counter

A stall that needs to know whether a neighboring stall's ingredient is ready asks that stall's own counter window rather than stepping behind the counter to look for itself. Every ingredient question between the two stalls goes through the same front-counter question, not a mix of asking and looking.

## Route-sheet handoff between delivery drivers

The route-sheet handoff between delivery drivers archive repeats dough-service, mozzarella-service, mozzarellaclient, readiness, batch, and boundary terms next to a paper route sheet passed hand to hand between two drivers changing shifts, a handoff habit between people rather than between any two kitchen services and unrelated to how one service reads another service's readiness state. The route sheet is discarded once the shift changeover is confirmed.

## A neighborhood must be posted before a counter can serve it

A counter only takes orders for the declared neighborhoods it has posted on its own board, even when a slip names a neighborhood served by a different counter nearby. An undeclared neighborhood is turned back before any box is packed.

## Same-minute arrivals still keep a fixed place in line

Two things happening at one moment keep a fixed place between them, rather than either slipping ahead or disappearing. Looking back later always finds each one exactly once.

## Kitchen queue display ticket reshuffle

The kitchen queue display ticket reshuffle archive describes the counter display reshuffling ticket numbers by estimated pickup time for customer convenience, a display-only reordering unrelated to how the order-history API paginates stored records or breaks ties in its cursor. The reshuffle never changes which ticket the kitchen prepares next.

## A retried try keeps its place in the same line

When something is retried, it stays linked to what came before instead of starting as something separate and unidentified. Looking back later always finds the same shared thread every time.

## A too-hot dial reading gets bounced back instead of rounded down

A dial reading past its safety margin gets sent back for a redo, not eased quietly into place. A ticket written at the wrong number stays flagged on the board until someone corrects it.

When the frying rack holds as many trays as its capacity allows, the line waves off any more trays from the coating table, guarded against by the rack's own limit rather than piling up out of sight. A waved-off tray waits at the coating table until there is room.
